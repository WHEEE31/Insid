01 · The problem

Maps end at the front door.

It's 15°F and snowing, and the fastest way to your next class is somehow the one route nobody has ever mapped. Turn-by-turn directions handle streets and parking lots without effort; the inside of the building — which floor, which stairwell, which door actually opens — is invisible to them.

The last serious attempt at solving that was in 2013.

02 · The solution

Insid

One walk becomes the map for everyone who follows it.

STEP 1

Record

A phone's motion tracking and barometer log a 3D path as someone walks: position, elevation, and floor — off hardware already in every pocket, with no beacons to install.

STEP 2

Align

Each walk locks onto the shared map through two known reference points, not a compass reading. Indoor magnetic fields bend by tens of degrees near rebar and elevator shafts — so the pipeline never asks for a heading it can't trust.

STEP 3

Route

Overlapping walks merge into one routing graph. Shortest-path search runs across it — across floors, across buildings — the same way it runs across streets.

The value compounds with bad weather instead of against it — sub-zero wind chill, a downpour, a package that can't get wet, a route that needs the elevator marked instead of assumed. None of it requires new infrastructure inside the building. It requires someone to have walked it once.

04 · Under the hood

Record raw. Align later.

Every layer of the pipeline can be swapped without asking anyone to walk the building again — the recorder never assumes which alignment strategy will win.

Crowdsourcing, visually

Three people, three walks down the same hallway. Independently noisy on their own; pulled into one shared frame once each is anchored.

Two tables. Every route comes out of them.

Postgres via Supabase. nodes is the shared registry; walks is one row per recorded path, points stored as ordered JSON.

Floors are pressure, not GPS

Floors show up as density peaks in barometric altitude — people dwell on a floor and only pass quickly through the gap between two of them, so a histogram of samples finds floors even through a continuous stairwell ramp.

Live, elsewhere

Covers select airports, malls, and stadiums. No coverage — and no announced plan for coverage — of college academic buildings.

WifiSlam
Shelved

Acquired by Apple for roughly $20M in 2013. Wi-Fi + inertial positioning, ~2.5m accuracy claimed. Folded into Apple's internal mapping work and never shipped as a standalone indoor-navigation product.

Ads, kept small on purpose

A wayfinding app doesn't need an aggressive ad load per user when usage is this frequent — every building, most days, for four years.

Campus licensing

A second line worth testing: direct licensing to facilities and accessibility offices, who already fund ADA-compliant wayfinding today.

06 · Scale

Every campus is the same problem, in a different building.

The pipeline doesn't know it's mapping Carnegie Mellon. It knows floors, nodes, and walks. Pointing it at a new campus is the same steps that produced the first five buildings: seed the node registry, walk the halls once, let the graph merge — no new hardware, no facilities partnership required to start.

The outside of every building is already mapped. Insid maps the inside — one walk at a time.
